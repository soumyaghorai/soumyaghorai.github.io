About

I am currently pursuing a Ph.D. (2022–present) in Power Electronics at IIT Kharagpur, under the supervision of Souvik Chattopadhyay. My research focuses on single-stage AC-DC, DC-AC, and AC-AC bidirectional isolated power electronic circuits that are capable of converting power with high efficiency, active power decoupling, modular power processing, and reduced passive components. Currently, I am focusing on dual-active-bridge (DAB) based single-stage circuits, hardware realization, implementation of control, and different modulation strategies.

Research Interests

My work explores single-stage isolated AC–DC/ AC-AC conversion circuits and the control and modulation strategies thereof, for EV and PHEV chargers, and SST applications , emphasizing soft switching enhancement, wide voltage range operation, active power decoupling, reduction of passive volume and high efficiency. A few highlights are as follows:

A single-phase integrated topology that combines a full-bridge PFC and DC-DC DAB converter inside a single-stage structure. The proposed modulation strategy exhibits a nearly flat efficiency curve over a wide range of input and output voltage and power variations, enabled by direct input current shaping and optimal dc bus control. The measured efficiency shows only a 3.2% variation across a wide operating range (150–450-V output, 110–210-V input, and 0.2–2-kW load) with a peak power stage efficiency of 97.2%. The proposed converter is also capable of pushing reactive power to the grid or supporting off-grid V2L (vehicle-to-load) unity and non-unity loads.

A carrier-based estimated power-mode control ( (EPMC)) technique is applied to decouple the twice-line-frequency pulsed input power and the DC output power in a single-phase, single-stage AC-DC dual-active-bridge (DAB) converter. In principle, the concept is built on conventional carrier-based voltage-mode and current-mode control techniques. The proposed technique updates the phase shift once per switching period, eventually regulating the power passing through the transformer to match a constant reference instantaneously. By employing a logic-driven, hardware-executed sampling architecture, the proposed active power decoupling (APD) technique provides instantaneous power decoupling without auxiliary circuits and with minimal decoupling capacitor requirements, while increasing the soft-switching percentage, thereby outperforming conventional APD approaches.
